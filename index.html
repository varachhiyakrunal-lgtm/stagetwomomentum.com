      What this test can and cannot tell you

      • Fills are today’s opening prices. Restricted-series stocks are priced on BSE, where Kite quotes them. A stock that opens at its upper price limit with no sellers might not fill in real life.
      • Costs are charged. 0.2% a side, as in the backtest. Tax, dividends and slippage on thinly traded stocks are not.
      • A few months prove little. These strategies make most of their money from a handful of large winners, so any single quarter can look great or terrible. Judge the record after a full year at least.
      • The same stock can sit in two portfolios. Several stocks appear in more than one, so the three are less independent than they look.
      • Trade-for-trade stocks (marked below) can be bought only for delivery and may hit price limits, so a real exit could take days.

              • The biggest finding: the early-stage numbers were partly luck of the calendar. Both rebalance every 21 sessions, and the original calendar happened to land on good days. Shifting it by a week or two cut early-stage from 33% to 26–28% a year, and early-stage + template from 31% to 22–23%. Splitting the money into three tranches a week apart removes that luck; the tranche figures are what to expect. Momentum v1 barely moved with the calendar (33.5–34.6%), so its result is sturdier.
              • Restricted-series stocks are now never bought. These trade-for-trade stocks can be bought only for delivery and often sit at their price limits, so a real portfolio cannot rely on getting in or out. History of the series comes from NSE bhavcopies to 23 September 2026, with Kite’s current list after that.
              • Momentum v1 now caps each holding at 15% of its tranche. With the other two changes, its worst calendar year improved from −34% to about −25% and the worst fall since 2019 from −48% to −37%.
              • Walk-forward check on early-stage + template. Choosing its stop (20/25/30%) and strength cut-off (70/80) each year using only earlier years returned 38.0% a year from 2012, against 38.2% for the fixed setting, so those two choices were not overfitted.
              • Not adopted: a volatility target (holding less when the portfolio is swinging hard) cut Momentum v1’s worst fall only a few points and cost about 4 points a year. A ₹1 crore/day liquidity floor cost 4–8 points a year: much of the edge is in thinly traded stocks.
              • Costs. Doubling trading costs to 0.4% a side lowered each strategy by about 1 to 1.5 points a year.
              • Idle cash belongs in a liquid fund: it adds about 0.5–1 point a year to early-stage and VCP. The figures above leave cash at zero return.

                  Read this before trading it

                  • Every version falls hard. Peak-to-trough losses run from 41% to 49%, and each one lost money in 2011, 2018 and 2025. The corrections fix where the 2024–26 gap came from; they do not remove losing years.
                  • The 35% figure is fragile. Early-stage momentum made 28% to 37% a year in the unseen period depending only on which day of the month it rebalanced. Plan on the lower end, before the data bias below.
                  • An early signal is a candidate, not a verdict. Price and volume cannot see debt reduction, margin expansion or a capex cycle ending. Half of all base breakouts go nowhere; the portfolio result comes from cutting those quickly and holding the few that run.
                  • Returns are overstated by missing dead stocks. The price file drops companies a year after delisting. Roughly 15% of 2014 Nifty 500 members and 5% of 2020 members have no data. The gap is wider among the smaller stocks the broad-market strategies trade, so trust their numbers less.
                  • Smaller stocks cost more to trade. The same per side is assumed everywhere. Stocks near the bottom of the 1,000 trade ₹1–2 crore a day, so real slippage will be higher and position sizes are limited.
                  • Dividends are excluded from strategies and benchmarks alike. Short-term capital gains tax is not deducted.
                  • Before 2014 the Nifty 500 universe is the 500 most-traded stocks, because index membership records start in 2014.
                  • VCP’s exit changed on 17 November 2026 from a daily close below the 50-day average with a 12% stop, to a weekly close below the 30-week average with a 20% stop. The old version stays on the Record tab for comparison. Of the exits tried, the 200-day daily average did slightly better over 16 years (29.2% a year, worst fall −31%); the 30-week one was chosen for its stronger 2024–26 record and weekly checking.
                  • All three switched to the 1,250 most-traded stocks on 17 November 2026. That roughly means companies worth ₹1,000 crore and more. The switch was made after seeing that it helped in 2019–26; before 2019 it changed nothing, because few small stocks traded enough. Momentum v1’s worst fall deepens to about −53% (it was −45% on the Nifty 500), and the robustness checks on the Record tab were run on the earlier 1,000-stock list. Missing data on delisted companies flatters the smallest stocks most, so this list is the one most exposed to that bias.
                  • Early-stage + template was dropped from the core strategies and the paper trade on 17 November 2026. It added little over plain early-stage momentum, held mostly the same stocks, and was the most dependent on its rebalance calendar. It stays on the Record tab for reference.
                  • The 25% stop on early-stage + template was picked after testing 20%, 25% and 30%. The three gave 28.9%, 30.6% and 28.9% a year and win rates of 42.9%, 43.8% and 44.9%, so the 25% result is partly luck. The same change on plain early-stage momentum lowered its return from 30.6% to 29.2%. Expect about 29%, and watch the paper-trade record.
                  • Sixteen years is one market history. variants were compared, so some of the edge is luck. Treat the test-period numbers as an upper bound.
